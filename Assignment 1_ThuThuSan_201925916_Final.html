Introduction¶

This project conveys the comparison between Myanmar export and import values between 2019 (pre-pandemic) and 2022 (post-pandemic and regime change in 2021) in US billion dollars. Since February 1, 2021, Myanmar military seized power from the democratic civilian government, leading to a series of mass protests and instability across the country (Nayt, T., Zin, K., Naung, N., 2025). Both pandemic and political instability have pushed Myanmar economy to the blink (BBC, 2021) .

The trading sector has been severely impacted because of these two double crises. The aim of the project is to highlight shift in trade values between partner countries for the years, 2019 and 2022. As the trading sector is vast, only top six partner countries based on trade values are analyzed in the report.

The dataset used in this project is referenced from World Integrated Trade Solution (WITS) website.

Data¶

The World Bank, in collaboration with the United Nations Conference on Trade and Development (UNCTAD), International Trade Center, United Nations Statistical Division (UNSD) and the World Trade Organization (WTO) — developed the World Integrated Trade Solution (WITS). The website allows users to access and download information on trade and tariffs for free (World Integrated Trade Solution, 1997).

The World Integrated Trade Solution (WITS) provides access to international merchandise trade, tariff and non-tariff measures data. Users can browse the country profile section to obtain countries exports, imports and tariff statistics along with relevant development data.

For this project, Myanmar's export and import data are selected to analyze the shift in trade values for 2019 and 2022. The dataset provides the complete picture of the trade sector such as number of exported/imported Harmonized System (HS) 6 digit products, export/import share in total products (%), trade balance and export/import values in US$. However, the project focuses only on the trade values of top six partner countries for the years, 2019 and 2022, with 2022 being the most recent year available on the WITS website.

Comparison of Top Six Export Partner Countries of Myanmar - 2019 vs 2022

The above bar chart analyzes Myanmar's top six export partners, comparing trade values between 2019 (pre-pandemic) and 2022 (post-pandemic and regime change) in billion US dollars. The data shows a significant overall downward trend.

The most critical finding is the collapse of trade with China, Myanmar's largest partner, which saw exports fall significantly from US 5.8 billion in 2019 to US 3.7 billion in 2022, a loss of US 2.1 billion.

A smaller decline of US 0.2 billion was also recorded with Japan. To counter this, trade with regional neighbors, Thailand and India, showed resilience and growth, increasing by US 0.5 billion and US 0.3 billion, respectively.

In conclusion, while Myanmar's exports were growing before 2020, the combined effects of the pandemic and the regime change have led to an overall contraction of trade, as regional gains were insufficient to offset the massive decline with its primary export destination, China.

Conclusion¶

The trade data from 2019 to 2022 shows that Myanmar's overall international trade volume has significantly contracted, with both total exports and imports showing a downward trend. This contraction was driven primarily by a major decline in trade with the key partner, China, across both import and export sectors.

While the larger markets fluctuated, Myanmar's trade structure shifted towards regional resilience. Trade with ASEAN neighbors, especially Singapore, Thailand, and India, showed necessary increases in certain categories such as imports from Singapore, exports to Thailand and India.

In summary, while regional relationships helped stabilize essential supply chains, these limited gains were insufficient to cover the massive loss of volume from the largest, most significant trade partner, which results in a negative overall economic impact.

Due to limitation in time and resources, only top six trading partners are analyzed in the project for 2019 and 2022, with 2022 being the most recent year available on the WITS website.

There are still scopes for future research, particularly in analyzing the effects of foreign policies, regional power conflicts, forthcoming national election and the possible regime change after January 2026 on Myanmar’s economy and trade.
